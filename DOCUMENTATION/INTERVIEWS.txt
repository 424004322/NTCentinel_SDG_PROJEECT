Simulated Transcript of Interview:

Stakeholder 1 (School Security Personnel):
“Being a security guard in the entrance places a lot of pressure, especially
the ratio of student to guard outweighs an individual capability. Checking for
violations, checking student’s bags, handing out logbooks, and verifying the
information consumes a lot of time which leads to a line that extends out on the
street. Furthermore, as lines form outside, students are placed in hazardous
areas; students complain which leads to chaos and eventually system failure.
Hence, improvements in the current process are essential for an efficient flow of
data.”
Stakeholder 2 (OSA Staff Members):
“As part of the Office of Student Affairs, it is our responsibility to monitor
student’s activities. Part of it is retrieving the logbooks from the security
personnel and deciphering the messy handwritings, then manually encoding it
into our tracking sheets. This current process poses a lot of potential human
errors and risks, especially that this logbook contains sensitive data of students
which authorized personnel should only have access to. Additionally, since this
data should be accurate, we expect that the guards are duly checking the
students information to have the system reflect the actual record and status of
students’ behavior and sanctions.”
